# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be straight with people, and be straight about the code.

This is a small sovereign project. It does not need a bureaucracy. It does need everyone
who touches it to be honest, direct, and decent, in that order.

## What we expect

**Toward people**

- Assume competence and good faith. Ask before you conclude.
- Critique the code, the claim, or the decision. Never the person.
- Disagreement is welcome and useful. Contempt is not.
- Respect the maintainer's time: say what you did, what you saw, and what you are asking
  for.

**Toward the work** (this part is not optional here)

- **Do not claim what you did not verify.** "I ran it and it passed, here is the output"
  beats "should work" every time.
- **Report the parts you could not check.** An honest gap is a contribution. A confident
  guess that turns out wrong costs someone a debugging session.
- **Do not weaken a check to make it pass.** A gate that cannot fail is worse than no gate,
  because people trust it.
- If you break something, say so early and plainly. Nobody here will be angry about a
  mistake that was reported.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment, personal attacks, discriminatory language, or sexualised attention in any
  project space.
- Publishing someone's private information.
- Deliberately misleading claims about what code does, what was tested, or what a security
  property guarantees.
- Introducing a backdoor, exfiltration path, or hidden data collection. This is
  instant and permanent removal, and it will be disclosed.

## Scope

Applies to this repository and every space where you act on its behalf: issues, pull
requests, commit messages, code review, and the SKCapstone coordination channels.

## Enforcement

Report a concern to the maintainers through the SKCapstone coordination channel, or
privately to the repo owner. For a **security** issue use `SECURITY.md` instead; do not
use a public issue.

Reports are handled privately. The maintainers may warn, revert a change, or remove a
contributor's access, in proportion to what happened and whether it repeats. Whoever is
the subject of a report gets to give their account before a decision, unless the situation
is an active risk to users.

Maintainers are held to this document too. A maintainer who ignores it should be called
out, and the call-out should be answered on the merits.

## Attribution

Written for this project rather than adopted wholesale. It borrows the spirit of the
Contributor Covenant on conduct, and adds the honesty rules this ecosystem runs on.
